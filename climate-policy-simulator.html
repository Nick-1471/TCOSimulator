Water Vapour vs CO₂, Tropics to Poles

This is an illustrative concept diagram, not a measured or modelled dataset. It exists to show the direction and interaction of three things: outgoing infrared radiation falls with distance from the equator, water vapour falls much faster still (warm air holds far more moisture than cold air), and CO₂'s concentration doesn't change with latitude at all. Drag the slider to see how the balance between the two gases shifts.

0° — Equator0°90° — Pole
Available outgoing infrared radiation Water vapour's effect CO₂'s effect
Illustrative only. Curve shapes are conceptual approximations (radiation intensity roughly tracking a cosine-of-latitude decline; water vapour declining faster still, consistent with the Clausius-Clapeyron relationship) chosen to show the correct qualitative behaviour, not measured or simulated climate data. The equator-to-pole starting ratio is set to match the commonly cited global-average split of water vapour contributing roughly 50% and CO₂ roughly 20% of the total greenhouse effect (Kiehl & Trenberth, 1997).
