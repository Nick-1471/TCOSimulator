Why this sector looks different from taxis and private cars

Two features of urban delivery duty shift the comparison in the EV's favour more than for either private cars or long-route taxis:

  • Stop-start driving penalises combustion vans more than their rated efficiency suggests. This model applies a real-world efficiency penalty to the ICE van to reflect that. The EV figure needs less correction, because regenerative braking recovers much of the energy stop-start driving would otherwise waste.
  • Depot charging is cheap and low-risk. A single overnight connection at an off-peak tariff avoids the public-charging premium entirely, unlike a taxi or private car that may depend on public infrastructure.
  • Battery mass reduces payload. Whether this matters depends on whether the route is volume-limited or mass-limited, set with the duty cycle toggle above.

How the model is built

  1. Capital. Purchase price minus resale value at the end of the horizon, using a type-specific resale curve. A hard-cliff battery replacement inserts a second vehicle purchase, net of salvage, when the effective lifespan is reached inside the horizon.
  2. Financing interest. A standard amortising loan at the chosen commercial fleet rate and term, isolated from capital depreciation.
  3. Energy. Fuel or electricity for the year's kilometres, using real-world efficiency for the ICE van (a stop-start penalty over its rated figure) and close to rated efficiency for the EV, since regenerative braking recovers much of that penalty.
  4. Depot charging infrastructure. A one-off capital line for the EV, separate from the vehicle purchase, so its effect on the total can be isolated from the alternative of relying on public charging.
  5. Maintenance, insurance and tyres. Annual amounts rising with general cost inflation, with tyre replacement on a distance-based interval that reflects each vehicle's weight and torque.
  6. Effective EV lifespan. The base lifespan slider is reduced automatically where public fast charging and climate extremes are both selected.
