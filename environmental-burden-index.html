South African Environmental Burden Index

A wider lens than CO₂ alone: tailpipe and grid air pollutants, water use and contamination risk, coal-mining impact, and the battery value chain — including the mining, refining and manufacturing impacts that occur outside South Africa. Every input below is adjustable; treat the results as a sanity-check starting point, not a verdict.
The Chamber's position: CO₂ is one row in this index, not the organising principle. A vehicle's real environmental footprint includes local air quality (PM2.5, SOx, NOx from both tailpipe and power station), Eastern Cape and Mpumalanga water stress, and a battery supply chain whose worst pollution and human-health impacts — cobalt mining in the DRC, nickel mining in Indonesia, cell manufacturing on coal-heavy grids in China — occur far from South Africa but are real costs of the vehicle, wherever they fall. Judging electrification on tailpipe CO₂ alone hides most of this picture in both directions: it overstates ICE vehicles' exhaust-pipe pollution relative to their upstream footprint, and it understates EVs' full lifecycle burden by stopping counting at the exhaust pipe they don't have.

Methodology & sources
  • Grid-side emissions (CO₂, SOx, NOx, PM, water) scale with the coal/nuclear/renewables/gas sliders above. Coal-plant factors are anchored to Eskom's own reported totals (≈1.6 Mt SO₂/yr, ≈804,000 t NOx/yr, ≈71 t particulates/yr — The Conversation, div. by an approximate 200 TWh/yr send-out) and to published water-intensity figures for wet- vs dry-cooled stations (historical average ≈1.44 L/kWh, dry-cooled Medupi/Matimba ≈0.1 L/kWh, wet-cooled ≈1.9 L/kWh — Renewable Matter). Nuclear and renewables factors are illustrative defaults pending Koeberg-specific figures; treat them as adjustable placeholders.
  • Tailpipe figures (CO₂, NOx, PM, SOx) use typical published emission-factor ranges per fuel and vehicle class, and are placeholders pending full South African drive-cycle citation — substitute your own figures where you have better data.
  • Non-exhaust particulate matter (tyre, brake and road wear) is modelled for every powertrain, not just ICE vehicles: independent research finds total PM10 roughly equal between EVs and modern ICE vehicles, with EVs only ≈1–3% lower on PM2.5, because EVs typically run ≈20–25% heavier than the equivalent ICE model (greencarcongress.com, citing 2016 study). This index applies a weight-based non-exhaust penalty to EV variants rather than crediting them with "zero particulates."
  • Battery value-chain pollution is scored from published battery-manufacturing carbon intensities (NMC811 ≈38 kg CO₂e/kWh vs LFP ≈15 kg CO₂e/kWh — electrive.com) and is explicitly not confined to South Africa: the breakdown panel under each EV powertrain attributes a share of the burden to cobalt mining (DRC), nickel mining/refining (Indonesia), and cell/pack manufacturing (predominantly China, on a coal-heavy grid), reflecting where those impacts actually occur. Air-quality and water-contamination evidence for DRC cobalt-mining communities (Lualaba/Kolwezi) is drawn from on-the-ground reporting: ≈98% of hourly air-quality readings near mining areas exceeded WHO PM thresholds, with a school and a community water source directly affected (globalvoices.org, Aug 2026). NMC811's heavier reliance on cobalt and nickel is scored above LFP on this dimension; LFP removes cobalt/nickel exposure entirely but at a lithium/iron-phosphate footprint of its own.
  • Battery disposal / end-of-life risk scales with battery size and chemistry. South Africa banned battery landfilling in 2021 but published, verifiable data on the country's actual current recycling *capacity* (as opposed to installed battery capacity, ≈1.3 GWh in 2023) could not be confirmed for this release — this is a disclosed data gap, not a modelled zero. Treat the disposal-risk score as a relative indicator (bigger battery, more cobalt/nickel = higher risk) rather than a measured quantity.
  • Water contamination risk and coal-mining impact are presented as adjustable 0–10 relative indices rather than false-precision physical units, because comparable per-kilometre South African figures do not exist in the public record. A specifically coal-related (rather than gold-mining) acid-mine-drainage figure for Mpumalanga could not be confirmed for this release — also a disclosed data gap — the index scores are directional placeholders pending that citation.
  • All figures are Chamber working assumptions for a sanity-check tool, not a certified life-cycle-assessment. Where your own data is better, override these defaults.
